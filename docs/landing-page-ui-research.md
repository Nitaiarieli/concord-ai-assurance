# Concord landing-page UI research

Date: 2026-08-21

## Research question

How should Concord compress a complex enterprise control loop into a short, credible, cinematic landing page without looking generic or implying unsupported product coverage?

## Current reference findings

| Reference | Observed strength | Principle applied to Concord |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Oak](https://www.oak.id/) | A single brand metaphor carries the hero, problem, product explanation, proof, and CTA. Short declarative copy alternates with concrete product imagery. | Keep one Concord world—source truth, underground lineage, damaged derivatives, repair, and verified growth—while alternating atmosphere with real assurance evidence. |
| [EverSwap](https://everswap.com/) | A very short narrative turns one mechanism into a spatial journey and explains audience value with minimal copy. | Use fewer chapters and let one evolving environment carry transitions instead of introducing a new layout for every idea. |
| [Alethia](https://www.alethia.earth/) | Technical credibility comes from measured outcomes, numbered methods, and visible verification. | Make proof states, read-back, coverage, exposure time, and exceptions more prominent than broad benefit claims. |
| [Nfinite Paper](https://nfinitepaper.com/#technology) | An invisible technical mechanism becomes understandable through a sequential transformation and precise performance evidence. | Present detect → trace → repair → verify → prove as one controlled sequence, with deeper mechanics available on demand. |
| [Son Daven](https://sondaven.com/en) | Chapter pacing, environmental continuity, and large editorial typography create memory without dense interface chrome. | Preserve the Concord chapter system and landscape continuity, but keep operational evidence immediately readable. |
| [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/) | The homepage explains a complex platform through one contextual graph and a small set of operational outcomes. | Keep one registered assurance boundary and avoid multiple competing diagrams. |
| [LangSmith](https://www.langchain.com/langsmith-platform) | A concise operational verb sequence—observe, evaluate, deploy—makes infrastructure understandable quickly. | Use five concrete Concord verbs and avoid category-level AI-governance language. |
| [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/cybersecurity/) | Enterprise positioning is paired with a direct outcome and one conversion path. | Keep the hero promise specific and make the final section drive one primary contact action. |
| [Linear](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh) | Its 2026 design update explicitly argues that structure should be felt rather than multiplied through unnecessary borders and separators. | Reduce visible sections and containers; use spacing, rhythm, and one evidence surface to organize the page. |
| [Credo AI](https://www.credo.ai/product) | Governance content is organized around evidence, risk, plans, and remediation rather than visual novelty alone. | Preserve evidence and remediation language, while differentiating Concord through downstream state reconciliation and behavioral verification. |

## Applied synthesis

1. Five visible chapters: promise, risk, control loop, enterprise proof, contact.
2. One hero promise with audience, mechanism, boundary, and CTA visible immediately.
3. One scroll-driven workflow rather than separate detect, lineage, remediation, and verification sections.
4. One enterprise proof chapter that combines stakeholder value, a traceable demonstration record, integration coverage, and commercial routes.
5. Product console, adapter registry, and readiness detail move into native expandable disclosures.
6. Motion is limited to source signals, registered lineage, repair propagation, and proof activation.
7. Unsupported, unresolved, accepted-risk, and planned states remain explicit and are never visually upgraded to verified.

## Rejected patterns

- Repeating the same promise in multiple full-height sections.
- Three-card SaaS benefit grids as the primary story.
- Decorative nodes, particles, gradients, or glass panels without product meaning.
- Universal graph or AI-governance claims.
- Metrics without customer-connected data or a clearly marked demonstration context.
- Multiple competing CTAs in the final chapter.
